Paradromics moves its brain-to-speech decoders onto Nvidia Holoscan with a 10-millisecond target. What the latency numbers mean for brain-computer interfaces

The implant is still in an early feasibility study with a handful of participants, but the compute roadmap now has dates and numbers, and latency is the metric that decides whether a synthetic voice feels like talking or like dictation.

Why it matters
ML engineersTwo pipelines: a direct neural-to-audio decoder with a sub-10 ms compute-plus-transport budget, and a text-first chain (decoder, language model, synthesis) under 100 ms. Online fine-tuning recalibrates the decoder in the background as signals drift; that is a continual-learning problem on a medical device.
ExecutivesNo deal terms were disclosed and nothing is cleared for sale. What changed is that Nvidia's medical edge stack (Holoscan, IGX, Jetson) becomes the standard environment for Paradromics' academic APEX partners, which is how platforms win in medical devices.
FinanceConnexus is investigational, enrolled in the FDA-approved Connect-One early feasibility study; the first commercial revenue is years away. Compare with Synchron, which announced its own Holoscan work in 2025, and Neuralink.
BeginnersA brain implant records signals when someone tries to speak; software turns those signals into a voice. Today the delay is noticeable. The goal is to make it feel instant.

Paradromics, the Austin company behind the Connexus brain-computer interface, said on Monday that it will build its development stack on Nvidia's Holoscan sensor-processing platform and Nvidia edge hardware, starting with speech and communication. The press release carries a quote from Nvidia's head of healthcare business development, David Niewolny, but is Paradromics-issued; no financial terms were disclosed and Nvidia published no separate announcement.

The numbers are the news. Paradromics set two engineering targets. For a direct brain-to-speech system, which maps neural activity straight to synthesised audio, the budget for compute and data transport is under 10 milliseconds. For the multi-stage route, in which a decoder produces text, a language model cleans it up and a synthesiser speaks it, the target is under 100 milliseconds. Chief scientific officer Vikash Gilja's point is that in conversation even small delays change the experience; people interrupt, overlap and respond to tone, and a system that lags by half a second turns dialogue into dictation. A third element, online fine-tuning, recalibrates the decoder in the background while the device is in use, because the neural signals an implant records drift over days and weeks and the alternative is repeated supervised retraining sessions.

The roadmap

For 2026 the plan is to port the speech decoders and synthesis pipelines to Holoscan, profile and reduce power consumption, and deploy Holoscan-ready Nvidia IGX systems to speech-focused partners in APEX, the Paradromics Application Expansion programme that gives academic groups a standard compute environment across institutions. In 2027 the work extends to high-degree-of-freedom motor control and to vision-language-action models that use what the user is looking at as context. The long-term goal is a smaller, lower-power external computer that someone can wear all day, which is where Nvidia's Jetson line comes in.

Connexus itself is an investigational device under evaluation in the Connect-One early feasibility study, registered as NCT07357428 with UC Davis as a site; it has FDA approval for the study, not for sale. Paradromics reported in September that its first participant, after using a mode that lets her compose in her own words, took a live phone call with her grandchildren. Enrolment numbers and performance data have not been published.

Why latency, not accuracy, is the headline

Accuracy numbers for speech BCIs have improved quickly in academic studies, and word-error rates in the single digits have been reported for text decoding. What has not been solved is the feel of the system: the lag between intent and sound, and the drift that degrades a calibrated decoder. Those are systems problems, and they are the ones a sensor-processing platform with a real-time scheduler is built for. Synchron announced Holoscan work in 2025; Paradromics is now on the same stack with public targets, which lets outsiders hold the company to them.

The counter-argument

Targets are not results, and a 10-millisecond compute budget says nothing about the end-to-end delay a listener experiences, which includes the time the decoder needs to accumulate enough signal to commit to a sound. The feasibility study is small by design, and the history of BCIs is full of impressive single-participant demonstrations that did not generalise. Nothing here moves the regulatory timeline.

What to watch

The first APEX deployments on IGX will show whether academic groups adopt the stack, which matters more for Paradromics' long-term position than any single demo. Measured end-to-end latency from a participant, not a compute budget, is the number to wait for. And watch whether Nvidia's medical edge platform starts appearing in other neurotech filings; a shared stack would make it easier for the FDA to evaluate software updates across devices.
