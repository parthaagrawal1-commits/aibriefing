DevelopingNvidia's halt to four Southeast Asian countries is the remote-access rule doing in a week what Commerce said would take 90 days

Dell and Supermicro have stopped fulfilling AI-server orders in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia; Chinese labs are already reporting delays, and nobody can say how to apply for the licence the rule requires.

Why it matters
ExecutivesCapacity contracted in the region is at risk of never being delivered. Get the provider's licence status in writing this week.
ML engineersChinese open-weight roadmaps just slipped by a quarter or two. Plan around GLM-5.4 and Kimi K3, not their successors.

The Financial Times reported on Thursday, citing three people familiar with the matter, that Nvidia has told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers containing its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Dell and Supermicro have told customers in those countries that Nvidia will not authorise their orders. The Wall Street Journal, in TechCrunch's account, framed the pause as Nvidia assessing whether its regional partners meet US export requirements. Neither report gives an end date, and the FT says it is unclear whether the suspension is temporary.

Why the four countries

The Bureau of Industry and Security's remote-access rule took effect on November 19. It requires a licence to ship advanced AI chips to those four countries and Brazil, and it closes the arrangement in which Chinese developers rented compute from data centres just across the border rather than importing chips they could not legally buy. The Institute for Progress estimates that offshore data centres of this kind supplied about 24% of China's AI computing power. The rule gives existing data-centre arrangements 90 days, until February 17, to obtain licences; the threshold, per Latham & Watkins, is roughly 760 H100-equivalents.

The scale of what is being paused is in Nvidia's own accounts. Its Singapore subsidiary booked $104bn of revenue in 2025, up 72% year on year, a figure that includes some Taiwan and China business routed through the unit but that still makes the city-state one of its largest booking centres.

What the halt changes

The rule was written with a grace period precisely so that operators could keep running while they applied. The halt shows that the vendors are not prepared to carry the risk of shipping into a licensing process that does not yet exist. A cloud-provider executive told the FT there is no guidance on execution and that nobody knows how to apply while the clock runs. Bloomberg reported on Wednesday that lenders have paused billions of dollars of financing for Asian data-centre projects linked to ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent, which follows the chip-compliance default clause Citi and JPMorgan reportedly wrote into a Bridge Data Centres loan last week. Singapore has said it wants a trusted route for its companies to keep buying; Malaysia's trade minister has told operators to comply.

The halt arrives three days after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive and created an independent National Security and Export Control Committee. Shares fell 3.9% to $149.30 on Wednesday after Monday's 31% rebound. Nvidia's $130bn guide for the current quarter signals that it expects lost Southeast Asian volume to be absorbed elsewhere.

The Chinese side of the ledger

Reuters reported the same day, citing two people, that Z.ai's GLM-5.5, a model of roughly 3 trillion parameters planned for November, could slip into the first quarter of 2027 or later. Z.ai shipped the 750B GLM-5.4 on November 23 instead. One source attributed part of the delay to increasingly effective anti-distillation safeguards at Anthropic and OpenAI; Anthropic's September report alleged more than 3.4 million suspected distillation exchanges from Zhipu over 17 days. Moonshot, which rents overseas compute and had been in talks to lease Blackwell clusters in Europe, could see its usual six-month gap between Kimi releases stretch to a year or more, with K4 landing around mid-2027. A Z.ai executive, Yang Haoyi, called the leaks fake news on WeChat without naming the model or the outlet; the company has published no release date. Tencent's Martin Lau said on his earnings call that the rule sets everyone in China back.

What to watch

  • Whether BIS publishes application guidance before February 17, and whether Nvidia resumes shipments to licensed operators or waits for the whole region to clear.
  • The first licence grants: which operators, which customers, and whether Chinese-linked tenants are excluded outright or capped.
  • Secondary-market prices for B300 servers in Shenzhen, quoted at $3.0-3.4m this week, as a gauge of how much capacity is stranded.
  • Chinese lab release dates. If GLM-5.5 and Kimi K4 slip as reported, the open-weight frontier for the next two quarters is Qwen, DeepSeek and the Western labs' small models.
  • Lender behaviour: paused project finance in Malaysia and Indonesia will show up in data-centre REIT and construction guidance before it shows up in Nvidia's numbers.
