DevelopingXPeng puts a date on its humanoid: IRON hardware production in January 2027, and driver assistance becomes a subscription

The robot walked on stage in Guangzhou and took questions; the commercially significant announcement was that new car buyers will pay monthly for autonomy from Q2 2027.

Why it matters
ExecutivesA carmaker just moved driver assistance from a one-time option to recurring revenue, worldwide, for new buyers from Q2 2027. Pre-order customers keep it for life, which is the cost of the transition.
ML engineersVLA 2.0 targets first global deliveries in H1 2027, with VLA Lite shipping in December on cars running two Orin-X chips. The interesting constraint is a vision-language-action stack running on last-generation inference hardware.
FinanceNo price for the subscription and two different launch dates for the robot from the same event. Treat the humanoid timeline as guidance, not a schedule, and watch the Guangzhou Auto Show on November 27.
BeginnersA humanoid robot that works without a human steering it by remote control is the claim being tested here. XPeng says the hardware goes into production in January and the robot goes on sale later in 2027.

XPeng held its annual AI Day in Guangzhou on November 25 under the theme "Origin", and IRON — the humanoid it has shown in some form for two years — walked out, took a microphone and answered questions. Asked how its intelligence compared with chief executive He Xiaopeng's, it said the two "each have our strengths". The company says it ran without remote control. The livestream passed five million views.

Stage demonstrations of humanoids are cheap. The dates are not, and XPeng gave several. IRON hardware goes into mass production in January 2027 on the line commissioned in September. The robot will do an internship as a sales associate in XPeng stores in the first quarter. It is 170 cm tall and weighs 62 kg. On when customers can buy one, the event produced two answers: XPeng's own presentation points to the second quarter of 2027, while a wire report from the afternoon session says February. IRON holds a press conference of its own at the Guangzhou Auto Show on November 27, which is where the discrepancy will probably resolve.

The announcement that changes a business model

From the second quarter of 2027, XPeng's Turing AI driver assistance becomes a subscription for new buyers worldwide. Customers who ordered before the change keep the features, in He's words, "for life". No price was given, and the event did not say which functions sit behind the subscription or whether it transfers with the car.

That is the structurally important part of the day. Tesla has sold Full Self-Driving as a one-time purchase and a subscription for years, and the resale-value problem — a feature bound to a buyer rather than a vehicle — has dogged it throughout. XPeng is making the switch after building an installed base that it now has to grandfather, which is the expensive order to do it in. The upside is a revenue line that compounds with fleet size rather than with quarterly deliveries, which is what investors have been pricing into Tesla for a decade and have never priced into a Chinese automaker.

The rest of the roadmap: VLA 2.0 targets first global deliveries in the first half of 2027, with VLA Adaptive Deceleration and Turing AI Co-Driving arriving in China in December on specific G9L and Mona trims, and VLA Lite in December on Max versions running two Orin-X chips. The 5C battery is being lifted to 6C over the air with no hardware change, cutting a 10–80% charge from about 12 minutes to 10.7. Driverless demonstration operations in selected areas are promised before the end of 2026, with the purpose-built YOYO-01 robotaxi in 2027; XPeng says it intends to supply the technology to partners rather than run fleets itself.

What to watch

The November 27 Auto Show appearance, which should settle whether IRON launches in February or the second quarter; the subscription price and whether it transfers with the vehicle, since that decides how European and Australian buyers react; whether the December VLA releases ship on the named trims on time, as that is the first dated promise to come due; and the end-of-year driverless demonstrations, because "selected areas" with no safety driver is a regulatory claim as much as a technical one. A robot that answers questions on stage is a demo. A production line in January and a price in February are checkable.
