SAP's reported 10,000-role cut would be its second in three years, and this time product and engineering are in the frame

Bloomberg says every SAP department has been asked to find reductions totalling at least 9% of staff; the 2024 round was framed as retraining, this one as rebuilding the software business around AI.

Why it matters
ExecutivesIf a vendor with SAP's margins thinks it needs 9% fewer people to build and sell software, your own IT and finance functions will be asked the same question by the board.
FinanceModel a charge against the 2024 precedent of about €3bn, and watch whether the German employment guarantee shifts cuts to the US and India.
DevelopersProduct and engineering are explicitly in scope. Expect module consolidation and slower support for anything outside the Joule roadmap.

Bloomberg reported on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the plans, that SAP is weighing the elimination of at least 10,000 positions as part of an overhaul of its software business around artificial intelligence. The people said the figure would be a minimum of 9% of the company's workforce of more than 110,000, that every department has been told to identify potential reductions, and that product, engineering and sales are under particular scrutiny. No final number has been set and the total could move in labour negotiations. SAP told Verdict it does not comment on speculation, that roles and functions naturally change over time, and that no decisions on further measures have been made.

The 2024 precedent

SAP has done this before, and recently. In January 2024 it announced a restructuring that cost about €3bn and affected roughly 8,000 roles, later widened to around 10,000, with most of the money going to voluntary leave and retraining programmes. The share price rose on the day; investors read it as margin expansion funded by a one-off charge. Employee trust in the board, measured in SAP's own surveys, fell during the programme and had not recovered by late 2025. Germany's works council secured an employment guarantee through 2028 earlier this year, which means a second round of this size would fall disproportionately outside Germany unless it is structured as voluntary departures.

What is different now is the framing. The 2024 programme was described as shifting people into growth areas, cloud and AI among them. The 2026 plan, as reported, is about the shape of the software business itself: fewer people writing, testing, documenting and selling software because agents do more of each. That is the same argument Workday's CFO made in reverse on Tuesday when he credited AI products with nearly $150m of new annual contract value, and it is why application-software stocks have been sold on good quarters all month.

Who leaves and who stays

Sales chief Thomas Saueressig is leaving to become chief executive of Ionos, and two of the six executive-board seats turn over in March. That matters because sales is one of the three functions named as under scrutiny, and because SAP's go-to-market has been built around large account teams selling multi-year S/4HANA migrations. If agentic features inside Joule are meant to drive expansion revenue without the same sales motion, the headcount model changes with it.

The counter-argument is that SAP's customers are mid-migration. The RISE and GROW programmes still depend on consultants, partner ecosystems and support staff, and the 2027 end of mainstream maintenance for older ECC systems is the moment when customers need the most hand-holding. Cutting engineering and support capacity a year before that deadline is a bet that AI tooling can absorb the load. Oracle, Microsoft and Salesforce are making versions of the same bet, and none has yet shown the productivity data to prove it.

What to do

  • If you are an SAP customer, ask your account team in writing which product areas are affected and what the support commitments are for modules outside the core cloud roadmap. Get it before the restructuring is announced, not after.
  • If you run a software or IT organisation, expect the question to come to you: SAP's reported ratio, roughly one role in eleven, will become a benchmark in board conversations regardless of whether SAP's numbers hold.
  • For investors, the signal to watch is the charge-to-savings ratio. The 2024 programme spent about €3bn for a stated goal of roughly €1bn of annual savings. A second round with a similar structure, announced into a market that is punishing software stocks, will be judged on whether the savings are redeployed into AI capex or returned.
  • For SAP staff outside Germany, the works-council guarantee does not apply. Voluntary programmes usually open first; the terms of the 2024 round are the template.
