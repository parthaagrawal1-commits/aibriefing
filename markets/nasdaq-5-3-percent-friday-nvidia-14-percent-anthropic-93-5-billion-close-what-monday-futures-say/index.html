DevelopingThe Nasdaq fell 5.3% on Friday and Nvidia 14% as the AI trade cracked; Anthropic closed a $93.5 billion IPO into the selloff. What the correction is pricing, and what Monday's futures say

Three indices in correction, a VIX at its highest since April 2025 and a 90% chance of a December rate rise: the week's real question is whether the AI build-out can still be financed at these prices.

Why it matters
FinanceFriday's move was a rates-plus-concentration event, not an earnings miss: the S&P's weekly loss of 8.6% was its worst since April 2025 with no change to any megacap's guidance. Watch the December 9 FOMC and the Dell and HPE prints this week for whether AI capex demand or financing costs give first.
ExecutivesYour vendors' cost of capital just rose. Expect harder negotiation on multi-year commitments and less appetite for the discounting that has defined 2026 AI pricing.
BeginnersA correction means an index is at least 10% below its recent high. It is not a crash, but it tells you investors have started asking how AI spending gets paid for, not just how fast it grows.

Friday was the day the AI trade stopped getting the benefit of the doubt. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 1,261 points, or 5.28%, to 22,617, its second-largest point fall on record, wiping out its gains for 2026. The S&P 500 lost 3.7% for a seventh straight losing session, its longest streak since February 2020, and ended the week down 8.6%. The Dow shed 950 points. All three indices now sit at least 10% below their highs, the textbook definition of a correction. The VIX closed at 35.89, its highest since April 2025. Nvidia fell 14.01% to $125.53.

What actually moved

No big company changed its guidance on Friday. What changed was the discount rate and the risk premium around a very concentrated trade. Philadelphia Fed president Anna Paulson said she was open to back-to-back rate rises and Governor Lisa Cook warned of falling behind inflation; prediction markets put the odds of a quarter-point increase at the December 9 meeting near 90%. On top of that sat a cluster of Nvidia-specific headlines: a Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining the company's accounting, California's attorney general confirming an investigative subpoena, and the continuing fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation, with the former chief executive due before three House committees on December 1. Add President Trump's Thursday threat of tremendous penalties on companies building data centres abroad, and the market had several reasons to sell the most crowded position it owned.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent offered the administration's reading on Sunday: the Fed risks a policy mistake if it over-tightens, Democratic hostility to AI is spooking investors, and the industry has done a terrible job explaining itself. By Monday morning the tape had steadied, with Nasdaq 100 futures up about 3.3%, Nvidia indicated around 8% higher and Anthropic about 3% higher before the open.

Anthropic's timing

Anthropic's IPO closed on Friday in the middle of all this. The prospectus shows 980 million Class A shares sold at $83, 956.4 million of them by the company, with a 30-day option for the banks to buy up to 147 million more, all from the company. Secondary reports say the option was exercised in full, which takes the deal to 1.127 billion shares and about $93.5 billion of gross proceeds, roughly $91.6 billion of it to Anthropic. A separate $26 billion private placement of Class N shares at the same price came from Google and Nvidia at about $10 billion each, AMD at $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft at about $1 billion each and TSMC at $500 million. The prospectus estimates net proceeds of about $117 billion with the option exercised.

The shares closed their first day at $97.17, up 17%, then fell 8.5% on Friday to $88.91, still 7% above the offer price. Reuters reported on Sunday that the process itself was unusual: a 22-bank syndicate led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley earned more than half a billion dollars in fees, some banks were capped at four senior bankers, and core deal-team members signed six-month agreements not to work with OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek or Hugging Face. Anthropic and its banks declined to comment.

The counter-argument

Bulls will note that nothing in the AI demand picture changed: Oracle said on Friday that financing for its OpenAI contract is in place, Nvidia guided to $130 billion for the current quarter only last week, and corrections in 2024 and 2025 were buying opportunities. The bear case is that this build-out is now funded by bonds, leases and vendor financing whose cost just went up, and that purchase commitments of $390 billion at Nvidia and roughly $895 billion at Anthropic were sized for cheaper money.

What to watch

HPE reports after the close today and Dell, HP Inc, Workday and Autodesk on Tuesday; listen for AI server backlog and whether memory costs are being passed through. December 1 brings Huang's closed-door House interview, December 9 the Fed decision. And keep an eye on Anthropic's lock-up terms in the prospectus: early releases tied to price thresholds are now a live question rather than a formality.
