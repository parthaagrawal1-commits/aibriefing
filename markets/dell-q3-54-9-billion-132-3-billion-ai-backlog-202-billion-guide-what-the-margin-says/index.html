Dell's revenue doubled to $54.9bn and its AI backlog hit $132.3bn. The margin is the number to read

AI servers earn mid-single-digit margins, Dell is repricing signed deals as memory costs climb, and infrastructure operating margin is guided below 15% next quarter.

Why it matters
FinanceRevenue +103% to $54.9bn against a $49.3bn consensus, adjusted EPS $9.45 against $6.59, and a third guidance raise to $202bn. Against that: AI servers carry mid-single-digit margins and ISG margin is guided just under 15% from 17.0%.
Executives$60.2bn of new AI orders in one quarter and a $132.3bn backlog means lead times, not budget, decide when you get accelerators. Dell is also raising prices after contracts are signed.
Security7,500+ AI Factory customers means 7,500+ new high-value estates. Procurement at this speed usually outruns the controls that are supposed to accompany it.
BeginnersBacklog is work customers have ordered but not received. Dell's is now larger than its entire annual revenue, which is why the stock reaction was muted.

Dell reported its October quarter on November 24 and the headline figures are the largest in the company's 42-year history: revenue up 103% to $54.9bn against an LSEG consensus of $49.3bn, GAAP net income up 266% to $5.7bn, adjusted earnings of $9.45 a share against $6.59 expected. The infrastructure group tripled to $42.0bn, with AI servers at $22.0bn, up 291%. Dell booked $60.2bn of AI orders in the quarter, lifting backlog to a record $132.3bn from $51.3bn in May, and raised its full-year revenue forecast to $202bn from $192bn — the third raise this year. The board added another $25bn to the buyback. The shares rose about 5% after hours, which for a 103% revenue beat is the tell.

Why the market shrugged

AI servers are a pass-through business with mid-single-digit operating margins, per Bloomberg's reporting on the quarter, and the mix is now dominated by them. Infrastructure operating margin was 17.0% this quarter against 12.4% a year ago, but Dell guided it to just below 15% next quarter. Revenue doubling while margin compresses is the arithmetic of a reseller in a supply squeeze, not of a company capturing the value it ships. Operating expenses at 7% of revenue, the lowest in company history, is the other side of the same story: there is no room to spend.

The cost line is memory. Dell told investors it expects the memory shortage to last into 2027, and the Financial Times has reported it is raising AI server prices after deals are signed — the practice we covered on Sunday, when Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro were reported to be seeking 25–45% above contract price at delivery. HPE said last week that memory and NAND stay constrained through at least the second half of 2028. Two of the three large server vendors are now telling customers, on the record, that a signed price is an estimate.

What the backlog actually promises

$132.3bn of backlog against a $78bn full-year AI server forecast means roughly twenty months of work at the current run rate, and most new orders are for Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 systems. That concentration cuts both ways. It is the clearest demand signal in enterprise hardware, and it also means Dell's delivery schedule is Nvidia's delivery schedule, its margin is set by memory pricing it does not control, and a cancellation window opens the moment a hyperscaler revises capital expenditure. Backlog is not revenue; XTX's $549m lawsuit against Dell over an AI server order, filed this month, is a reminder of what happens when the two sides disagree about what a booked order committed to.

Chief operating officer Jeff Clarke's line — "We are now a different company" — is accurate and ambiguous. The PC business grew 3% to $12.9bn and consumer fell 15%. Dell is now an AI infrastructure integrator with a legacy client division attached, and it is being valued as one.

What CFOs should do

If you have accelerators on order, confirm in writing whether your price is fixed or indexed to component cost, and get the delivery window in a schedule rather than a sales email; two vendors have now signalled they will revisit both. Model your 2027 AI hardware line with a 25% price band rather than a point estimate. If you are deciding between buying and renting capacity, the repricing risk now sits on the buy side of that comparison in a way it did not in September — rented capacity has its own exposure, but the contract is shorter. And if you hold the stock, watch the ISG margin line next quarter rather than the backlog headline: 15% guided against 17% reported is the number that moved.

What to watch

Dell's January-quarter ISG margin against the sub-15% guide; whether the $23.5bn of AI server revenue guided for this quarter converts without further price renegotiation; Nvidia's Vera Rubin shipment cadence, since most of the backlog depends on it; and whether any large customer publicly disputes a repricing, which is how this becomes a legal question rather than a procurement one.
