DevelopingAnthropic prices at $83 and raises about $81 billion, the largest IPO ever. What the final terms say, and what to watch when ANT opens today

The price landed $5 above the top of the range and the share count grew, which tells you how the book filled; the prospectus numbers tell you what the buyers are underwriting.

Why it matters
FinanceThree numbers to carry into the open: $83, 980 million shares, and a 147-million-share greenshoe. If the stock trades well above $83 the banks exercise the option and the raise approaches $93 billion; if it trades below, they buy stock back to support it.
ExecutivesYour vendor's cost of capital just fell and its disclosure obligations just rose. Both will show up in how it negotiates with you next year.
BeginnersAn IPO is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time. Anthropic just did the biggest one in history.

Anthropic's final terms arrived late on Wednesday: 980 million Class A shares at $83 each, against a marketed range of $70 to $78. The company itself sold 956,351,354 shares and a group of existing holders sold 23,648,646, and the underwriters, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, hold a 30-day option on a further 147 million shares. Arithmetic on the announced figures gives a base raise of about $81.3 billion, roughly $79.4 billion of it to the company, which passes the $85.7 billion SpaceX listing once either the option or the placement is counted, and which several outlets are already calling the largest IPO ever. Bloomberg's reported total of $107.3 billion adds the private placement that Nvidia, Google and other partners agreed earlier in the roadshow. Shares begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market today under the ticker ANT, and the offering closes on Friday.

How the book filled

Two things moved during the roadshow and both point the same way. The price went up $5 past the top of the range, and the share count rose above the 825 million first marketed. Companies do not usually expand a deal and lift the price at once unless demand is many times the available stock; the book was reported to be covered about six times before pricing, and a Japan tranche alone reportedly took about $3 billion. Pre-IPO derivatives on Hyperliquid had the shares near $96, though those markets are thin.

What buyers are underwriting

The amended prospectus is the document that matters. Revenue was about $17 billion in the third quarter, up from $11.6 billion in the second, and $33.2 billion for the nine months to September 30. Full-year 2025 revenue was $2.5 billion. The third quarter carried an operating loss of $567 million after a first operating profit of $974 million in the quarter before, and the company discloses about $895 billion of computing commitments, roughly $70 billion of them due within a year. At $83 the fully diluted value has been reported at around $1.5 trillion, which is in the region of 22 times the current quarterly revenue run-rate annualised, a multiple that assumes growth continues at something like the pace of the past year.

Ownership after the deal is concentrated. Amazon is reported to hold about 15% and Google about 14%, a combined stake above 29%, and a single Class F share held by an entity owned by the seven co-founders carries 50.1% of the vote on most matters, so Anthropic lists as a controlled company. Spark Capital, the only venture firm named in the filing, bought about 12.2 million preferred shares for roughly $777 million between 2023 and 2026; those convert to about 124 million common shares worth more than $10 billion at the offer price.

The safety disclosure

One risk factor has drawn more attention than the rest. The amended filing states that the company has reached the research-and-development acceleration threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy, the point at which AI contribution to internal progress could compress two years of work into one, and that triggering it obliges Anthropic to assess and add safeguards rather than pause. It also says humans remain in control and that insurance could be insufficient after a catastrophic incident. Public investors will now price that language every quarter.

What to watch

The first print is the least informative number of the day; the close is better, and the level the stock finds relative to the $26 billion strategic placement, which was struck at the range price, is better still. Watch whether the greenshoe is exercised in full, which the banks will do only if the stock holds above $83. Watch the lock-up calendar, since the first large unlock will set a ceiling months from now. And watch Salesforce, ServiceNow and the other software names that sold off on Tuesday on the theory that this listing reprices their growth; a strong debut will test whether that was a rotation or a repricing. The company's first earnings call as a public company, likely in February, is when the $895 billion of commitments gets a quarterly cash-flow number against it.
