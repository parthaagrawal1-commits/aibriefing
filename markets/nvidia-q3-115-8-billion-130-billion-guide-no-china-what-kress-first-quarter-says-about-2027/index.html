Nvidia's first quarter without Huang: $115.8 billion in, $130 billion guided, zero China assumed and a margin that falls to 71%. What the numbers say about 2027

The beat was expected; the shape of the guide was not. Nvidia is now telling investors that the next quarter will be bigger, less profitable per dollar and entirely ex-China, and that the founder is not coming back.

Why it matters
FinanceThree numbers to carry into your model: 71% to 72% gross margin, $390 billion of purchase commitments, and no China compute revenue. The first two are new; the third was implicit and is now official.
ExecutivesA $130 billion quarter from a company with an interim CEO means the supply you are counting on for 2027 is real but the person who signs allocation letters may change by spring.
ML engineersKress's one technical sentence was that Vera Rubin is 'off to an explosive start' and the roadmap is on track. That is the first on-record confirmation since the leadership change.

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended October 25, up 103% on a year earlier and 20% on the previous quarter, with non-GAAP earnings of $2.68 a share against the $2.47 analysts expected and a 74.9% non-GAAP gross margin. Data-centre revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110% year on year; the segment Nvidia now calls edge computing added $8.2 billion. It was the first set of results presented by Colette Kress, chief financial officer and interim chief executive since Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, and the first in which the company's forecast assumed nothing at all from China.

The guide, not the beat

Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $130 billion plus or minus 3%, a figure that includes a fifty-third week in the fiscal calendar. The gross-margin outlook of 71% to 72% is the detail that matters. It is roughly three points below the quarter just reported, and at a $130 billion run rate three points is about $4 billion of gross profit a quarter that will go to memory suppliers rather than to Nvidia. Kress attributed the drop to higher component costs; high-bandwidth memory is the component in shortest supply, and its price is rising faster than Nvidia's.

The press release states that the outlook assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. Tech Times, citing the call, reports that Kress said Nvidia is no longer shipping licensed Hopper products there, and that Jefferies puts the annual revenue forgone at $44 billion to $62 billion. Those figures are secondary; the policy fact is primary. The remote-access rule published by the Commerce Department on Monday closes the route by which Chinese customers rented Nvidia chips abroad, and Nvidia has chosen to forecast as if that route is gone for good.

The commitments

Purchase commitments, the contracts Nvidia has signed with its own suppliers, rose to $390 billion, up from the $279 billion disclosed in July. Neocloud commitments, the capacity Nvidia has agreed to buy back from the rental providers it invests in, stood at $32 billion. Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter and has $227.3 billion of repurchase authorisation remaining after the $150 billion increase approved in September; the company says it intends to use all of it by the end of fiscal 2028.

Read together, the numbers describe a company spending its balance sheet to lock in supply for a demand curve it believes is still steepening, while using the cash left over to support its own share price through a leadership crisis. Kress raised the annual growth forecast to 75% from 70% and said demand is accelerating.

The CEO question

Chair Harvey Jones said on the call that the board does not intend to bring Huang back and that the search for a permanent chief executive, covering internal and external candidates, will not be rushed. That ends a week of reports about allies lobbying for a return. Kress declined to discuss the circumstances of the departure. Nvidia's shares, which closed Monday at their lowest level since June 2025 and about 35% below the October 6 record, rose around 4% in after-hours trading.

The counter-argument

A guide that beats consensus by $20 billion while margins fall is the pattern that preceded every previous semiconductor peak: volume up, pricing power down. SemiAnalysis is reported to have cut its 2028 accelerator-spend forecast by 28% last week on slower revenue growth at frontier labs and tighter project financing. Nvidia's $390 billion of purchase commitments are a bet against that view, and if it is wrong the inventory charge would be historic. The Anthropic IPO prices tonight on exactly this question.

What to watch

The Senate Banking hearing on December 3, at which Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick have been asked to testify about export controls. Whether the permanent CEO is named before the next report in February and whether it is an insider. The first Vera Rubin volume-shipment disclosure. And the gross-margin line next quarter: if it lands below 71%, the memory suppliers, not Nvidia, are winning the cycle.
