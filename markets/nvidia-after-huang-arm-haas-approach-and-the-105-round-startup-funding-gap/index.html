DevelopingNvidia approached Arm's Rene Haas, FT reports, as VCs count the 105 startup rounds Huang's Nvidia joined this year

The succession search is weeks from a decision at best; the money Nvidia poured into neoclouds and model labs under Huang may change faster than the CEO's office does.

Why it matters
FinanceHaas would need to exit Arm, SoftBank's board and SoftBank Group International. Treat the search as an overhang through at least the November 17 call, and watch Arm's reaction as the second-order trade.
ExecutivesIf a critical supplier or neocloud partner raised from Nvidia in the last two years, ask this week how much of its next round depended on Nvidia returning.
ML engineersNothing about CUDA, Blackwell or Rubin changes with the name on the door. What may change is Nvidia's willingness to fund the open-source and startup ecosystem that made CUDA the default.

Two stories about Nvidia ran on Friday, and the second matters more to most readers than the first. The Financial Times reported that Nvidia's board has approached Rene Haas, chief executive of Arm, as a possible successor to Jensen Huang; Reuters could not verify it, Arm declined to comment, and Nvidia said it is still selecting a search firm and that speculation about any candidate, internal or external, is premature. Separately, TechCrunch gathered venture investors who put a number on what Huang's departure removes from the startup market: Nvidia took part in 105 funding rounds this year, often as the largest cheque.

The Haas approach, and why it is slow

Haas, 64, is a plausible name because he is a former Nvidia executive. He joined in 2006, ran the computing products business for seven years, moved to Arm in 2013 and has been its CEO since February 2022, taking it public on Nasdaq in September 2023 at roughly $54.5 billion. He also sits on SoftBank's board and since April has run SoftBank Group International, the unit that holds SoftBank's AI bets. Any move to Nvidia would mean untangling all three.

The FT described him as one of several candidates with no guarantee of an offer or an acceptance. Analysts quoted by Decrypt have also floated Nvidia's data-centre head Ian Buck and AMD's Lisa Su; neither is more than a name on a list. Inside Nvidia, Wired reported, a suggestion-box thread has drawn more than 1,600 comments, many asking the board to bring Huang back. That is a measure of staff sentiment, not of board intent. Arm's US shares closed down more than 5% and Nvidia's more than 3%.

The funding gap is already open

Under Huang, Nvidia behaved like the largest AI venture fund in the world. TechCrunch's reporting lists CoreWeave, Crusoe (including a $1.4 billion Series E), Mistral, Lambda, Black Forest Labs and an $800 million cheque into Reflection AI's $2 billion Series B before that company had meaningful revenue, alongside the pending $13 billion Hugging Face acquisition expected to close in 2027. Investors described a pattern: an infrastructure startup needing $100 million to $500 million of GPUs could raise roughly half of it from Nvidia at a $1 billion-plus valuation, and the deal locked the startup into Nvidia hardware.

Several investors said those decisions ran through Huang personally and that interim CEO Colette Kress is seen as more conservative. The expectation is smaller follow-on cheques and a narrower set of new bets. Battery Ventures' Dharmesh Thakker doubted Google, Amazon, Microsoft or AMD would write hundreds of millions into pre-revenue infrastructure companies the way Nvidia did; others argued the ecosystem is now bigger than one person.

The counter-argument

Nvidia's 8-K promised no change to the roadmap or customer commitments, and a board that just pushed out a founder over governance is unlikely to announce a strategy shift before earnings. Kress has co-signed every one of those investments as CFO. The venture pullback is an expectation, not an announcement, and Nvidia still has the balance sheet to keep investing if it chooses. The $500 billion AI data-centre financing pact announced in August has not produced deals, but that was true before Huang left.

What to watch

Tuesday's third-quarter call is the first with Kress alone and the first since Anthropic's prospectus disclosed a $10 billion Nvidia commitment; listen for the phrase 'strategic investments' in the guidance. Watch whether any Nvidia-backed neocloud reprices or delays a round in the next month. And watch Arm: if Haas is named, SoftBank has a succession problem of its own, and Nvidia would be run by someone whose last decade was spent making CPUs matter in AI servers.
