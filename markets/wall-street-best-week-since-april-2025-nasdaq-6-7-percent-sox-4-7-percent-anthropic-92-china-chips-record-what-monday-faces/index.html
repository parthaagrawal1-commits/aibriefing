Wall Street's best week since April 2025: the S&P gained 5.6%, memory stocks led, Anthropic hit $91.88, and Nvidia sat out Friday

A shortened Black Friday session capped a rebound that reversed most of the prior week's 8.6% drop, but the leaders were memory and chip-equipment names rather than Nvidia, which closed 8.8% below Monday with its credit rating on watch.

Why it matters
FinanceThe S&P is still 6.7% below its October 6 record and the Nasdaq 12.6% below. A 5.6% week after an 8.6% drop is a half-recovery, priced with a Fed hike on December 9 near certain.
ExecutivesMemory led the rally because memory is the shortage. If your 2027 plan assumes server prices fall, this week's tape says the opposite.
ML engineersSanDisk +16%, Micron +7.6%, Seagate +7.9%, Western Digital +7.2%: the market is paying for storage and HBM capacity, which is what gates inference clusters next year.

US stocks finished a holiday-shortened Friday with the broadest rally since the spring of 2025. The S&P 500 rose 1.60% to 7,297.49, the Dow Jones Industrial Average 1.18% to 50,774.94 and the Nasdaq Composite 2.31% to 24,129.50 in a session that closed at 1 p.m. Eastern. For the week the S&P gained 5.60%, its best since the week ended April 11, 2025, the Dow 5.46% and the Nasdaq 6.68%. More than four in five S&P stocks rose on Friday and all eleven sectors closed higher.

The context matters. The week ended November 20 saw the S&P fall 8.61%, a correction driven by Nvidia's post-earnings slide, the resignation and then reinstatement of Jensen Huang, and the first week of the Commerce Department's remote-access rule on advanced chips. Monday's reinstatement rally added 4.73% to the S&P and 31.5% to Nvidia; Tuesday and Wednesday gave some of it back. Friday's gain still leaves the S&P 6.67% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93 and the Nasdaq 12.57% below its own.

Who led

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 4.69% on Friday, but the leadership was not the usual one. SanDisk gained 15.96%, Micron 7.60%, Seagate 7.90% and Western Digital 7.22%; Coherent and Lumentum, which make optical interconnects, rose 11.92% and 7.98%. Marvell added 7.34%, AMD 4.56%, Broadcom 3.82%. Nvidia rose 0.80% to $150.49, which left it 8.8% below Monday's close of $165.05 even after a 19.9% week. S&P Global placed Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch with negative implications on Wednesday, Moody's having already moved its outlook to stable on November 18, and Bloomberg's Friday report that an export-restricted GB300 rack had been documented at a Shanghai data centre arrived before the open.

Anthropic, public since November 19 at $83 a share, rose 10.98% to $91.88 from $82.79 on Wednesday, its first close meaningfully above the IPO price after dipping to $81.91 on Tuesday. Tesla gained 5.22%, Amazon 3.96% and Meta 3.13%.

The move was global. China's CSI Semiconductor index posted its largest one-day gain on record, up roughly 17.5%, and the STAR board rose 14% after a rare midday State Council statement pledging support for the market; Hong Kong, Seoul and Tokyo all closed higher. Brazilian assets jumped on the naming of Bradesco's chairman as finance minister.

What the rally is not

It is not a change in the rate picture. Wednesday's core PCE print of 3.3% and the upward revision of third-quarter GDP to 4.3% pushed traders to price a quarter-point Fed hike at the December 8-9 meeting as close to certain, and the 10-year Treasury yield sits near 4.9%. Equity multiples rose this week into tighter money, which is why the VIX, at 23.81, is still well above the teens even after falling from 35.89.

It is also not a verdict on AI capital spending. The Bank of England's Financial Stability Report on Thursday put hyperscaler bond issuance above $305bn this year and total AI-linked exposure above $4tn; nothing in this week's price action addresses whether that debt is well placed. What the leadership does say is that investors expect the memory and storage shortage to persist: HPE and Dell both guided to memory constraints through 2028 this month, and the market is paying the suppliers accordingly.

What to watch

  • December 3. Jensen Huang testifies before the Senate Banking Committee on export controls, days after Bloomberg's Shanghai report. Nvidia's week was made on Monday; the hearing decides whether the second week looks like the first.
  • December 8-9. The FOMC. A hike is priced; the statement's language on 2027 is not.
  • November 30 and December 1. Promotional API prices end at OpenAI (GPT-5.6 Sol) and Google (Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon). Small numbers, but they are the first scheduled price rises in a market that has only cut.
  • Anthropic's first lock-up expiry and its first quarter as a public company will set the comparables for every private AI valuation. At $91.88 it trades 11% above its IPO price and roughly in line with the $1.5tn reference valuation.
  • Memory pricing. If DRAM and NAND contract prices keep rising into Q1, the names that led this week keep leading, and the cost of every inference cluster keeps rising with them.
