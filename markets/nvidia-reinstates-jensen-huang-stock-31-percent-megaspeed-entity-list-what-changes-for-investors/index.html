DevelopingNvidia reinstates Jensen Huang and the stock jumps 31% to $165; Commerce blacklists Megaspeed the same day. What changed for investors, and what has not

A unanimous board vote, a White House push and a new export-control committee put Huang back in charge eleven days after he resigned, but the congressional interviews, the government inquiries and the Megaspeed question are all still open.

Why it matters
FinanceThe 31% move recovered most of the two-week drawdown, but it was a short-covering day with the government inquiries, the Dec 1–3 testimony and a new compliance structure still ahead. Position for headline risk, not for resolution.
ExecutivesNvidia now has an independent export-control committee and is hiring a chief compliance officer. If you buy or resell accelerators, expect more end-user questions, not fewer.
SecurityThe Megaspeed listing names seven entities across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand. Check your cloud and colocation suppliers' affiliates against the Entity List before the Federal Register notice lands on Wednesday.

Nvidia's board said on Monday that it had voted unanimously to return Jensen Huang to the roles of president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, eleven days after he resigned on November 12. The statement says the board was satisfied with Huang's candour and his ability to lead, and that it had received input from senior levels of the United States government that left it confident his return poses no national security risk. Colette Kress, who served as interim chief executive, goes back to being chief financial officer.

The stock closed at $165.05, up $39.52 or 31.5%, adding close to a trillion dollars of market value in a session and pulling the whole market with it: the S&P 500 gained 4.7%, the Nasdaq Composite 6.5% and the Dow 3.0%, the best day for all three since April 2025 and a sharp reversal of Friday's correction. Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that President Trump and Vice President Vance intervened personally over the weekend because they saw Huang as the one executive protecting American open-weight models, a view that fits Nvidia's August agreement to buy Hugging Face and its backing of Reflection AI.

What the board actually changed

Three structural things came with the reinstatement. First, the investigation into the reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort continues under a committee of independent directors with its own counsel, and the board says it will run to a conclusion. Second, a new National Security and Export Control Committee, again independent directors only, now oversees trade compliance and is engaging counsel with national security experience. Third, Nvidia is creating a chief compliance officer role reporting to the nominating and governance committee. None of that existed a month ago, and all of it will shape how the company answers end-user questions about where its chips go.

Megaspeed, the same afternoon

Hours after the Nvidia announcement, the Commerce Department posted an Entity List rule for public inspection that adds seven companies in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, led by Megaspeed International, the firm at the centre of the allegations that compute and chips were routed to Chinese customers. The others include Apex Enterprise Solutions, an Alibaba subsidiary that Alibaba says was listed in error, and affiliates named Swiftdata, Speedmatrix, PT Megaspeed International, MSFusion and Megaspeed (Thailand). Licences will be required with a presumption of denial. Formal publication in the Federal Register is scheduled for Wednesday, November 25. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing and says its former chief executive left in August 2025; Singapore's prime minister says its own investigation continues.

The calendar has not moved

The chairmen of the House Foreign Affairs, China Select and Intelligence committees said on Monday that Huang and general counsel Tim Teter will still sit for transcribed interviews on December 1 and 2, and that reinstatement resolves none of their national security concerns. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott separately confirmed a public hearing on December 3, and Senator Warren says the board still owes answers on export-control compliance. Nvidia says it will keep cooperating with government investigations and inquiries, plural.

The counter-argument

Nomura's Charlie McElligott called Monday a mechanical bounce, driven by short covering and options hedging rather than new information about demand. That is fair: nothing about data-centre orders changed, and the company's China revenue is still zero in its own guidance. What changed is the removal of one tail risk, an Nvidia without its founder, while leaving the others in place.

What to watch

The Federal Register notice on Wednesday will show whether the Entity List additions carry any savings clause for shipments already in transit. The December 1–3 appearances are the next scheduled catalyst, and the first public statements from the new compliance structure will tell resellers how much more paperwork is coming. Treat the stock's recovery as the market pricing out a governance vacuum, not pricing in a settlement.
