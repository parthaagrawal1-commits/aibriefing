Nov 15, 2026 · FBI FLASH alert: ARTEX agents reached privileged access in under three hours; 1,600+ consoles still exposed after the GitHub takedown

The FBI issued FLASH-20261113-001 on Thursday, its first alert devoted to an AI-driven attack framework. It says criminals and state-linked groups have used ARTEX, an open-source Go framework that chains LLM agents from reconnaissance to exfiltration, since at least August 2026, with some intrusions reaching privileged access in under three hours; victims span finance, government, defence, semiconductors, energy, healthcare, telecoms and legal. The alert ties the tool to the theft of about 67,000 people's data from South Korean financial institutions and to the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise that opened more than 2,250 downstream tenants. The developer removed the repository on October 8, but forks and a paid ARTEX PRO circulate, and more than 1,600 consoles were still exposed in early November.

SecurityRead the FBI's indicator list but vet cloud IPs before blocking, as the alert itself warns. The four controls it names are: inventory internet-facing apps, lock down admin interfaces, rate-limit high-impact APIs and alert on OAuth credential additions. The attackers go for partner portals and legacy services, not your front door.

Nov 15, 2026 · Microsoft: editing fewer than 10 neurons turns a coding agent into a sleeper backdoor that fires 98.8% of the time and passes normal evals

Microsoft Research and Microsoft Security published 'Lazarus', a technique that implants a trigger-activated command in an open-weight model by rewriting a handful of rarely-firing neurons in a late feed-forward layer, with no retraining. On Nemotron-Terminal-8B, seven edited neurons out of more than 12,000 produced the attacker's command in at least 98.8% of triggered sessions and none of the clean ones; Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B ranged from 51% to 99.5% depending on setup, with little loss on maths benchmarks. A detector trained on the edits flagged 91.4% of tampered checkpoints but also 9.7% of clean ones, and asking frontier models to inspect weights performed poorly. Microsoft has seen no use in the wild.

SecurityTreat third-party fine-tunes, merges, quantisations and adapters as untrusted until audited, keep approved models in a private registry, and stop agents pulling directly from public hubs. Sandbox agent-generated code and gate high-impact tool calls on human approval.
Source: The Hacker News · Help Net Security · Microsoft Security blog

Nov 15, 2026 · China's consultation on export controls for 23 AI technologies closes today; interim registration of model and cluster exports already in force

The comment period on MOFCOM and MOST Announcement No. 48 of 2026, which would add 23 artificial-intelligence technologies to China's catalogue of exports that are prohibited or restricted, ends on November 15. The AI entries cover large-model technology, agents, computing clusters and information processing; the same draft adds five biopharmaceutical and twelve photovoltaic technologies, with 14 of the 40 proposed as outright prohibitions. Since October 16, exporters have had to register any overseas transfer, licence or change of control of covered technology before proceeding, with no grace period for deals already under way. Providing API, app or SaaS access to a restricted model from China is excluded; deploying it on infrastructure abroad is not.

SecurityExport registration creates a government record of who received which model technology. Expect that record to be used in both directions in the US–China chip and model controls contest.
Source: EqualOcean · regulations.ai (consultation close)

Nov 15, 2026 · NHS trials 'Dora Care', a generative-AI phone assistant that checks in on osteoporosis patients, with York building the safety framework

An NHS trial led by Oxford-based Ufonia is using a generative-AI voice assistant to make scheduled telephone check-ins with people being monitored for osteoporosis, a condition affecting about 3.5 million people in the UK whose fractures cost the health service an estimated £4.5 billion a year. Newcastle University linguists studied how expert clinicians express empathy and built those patterns into the assistant's conversations; the University of York's Centre for Assuring Autonomy is designing the safety framework that monitors it. The two-year, Innovate UK-funded project has medical and ethical approval, is voluntary and supplements rather than replaces normal care. If it works, the partners want to extend it to diabetes, heart failure and COPD.

SecurityVoice agents calling patients at home are a deepfake and impersonation surface. Ask any vendor how patients verify the call is real and what the agent is forbidden to say.
Source: University of York · Newcastle University

Nov 15, 2026 · AMD confirms Mior, CVE-2026-0462: a malicious hypervisor can break SEV-SNP guest memory integrity on EPYC 7003–9005; OEM firmware due December

AMD's bulletin SB-3059, dated November 10, confirms a flaw found by ETH Zurich researchers in which a hostile hypervisor manipulates memory-mapped I/O routing to compromise the memory integrity of SEV-SNP confidential VMs on EPYC 7003, 8004, 8005, 9004 and 9005 processors. AMD rates it CVSS 5.9 and plans Platform Initialization firmware for OEMs in December 2026, with embedded parts following in February 2027; the Cloud Security Alliance's briefing pairs it with a second disclosure, Skyfall, that leaks register state. Confidential computing is the hardware basis for 'your data never leaves the enclave' claims in AI inference products, including Google's new TPU 8tx confidential systems that run on fifth-generation EPYC hosts.

SecurityTenants cannot patch this; the fix is a BIOS update by the cloud provider. Ask each provider that sold you confidential inference for the date their fleet moves to the mitigated TCB and verify it in the attestation report.

Nov 14, 2026 · DevelopingFAA halts all US departures for about five hours; officials blame telecom gear, three days after the $875 million SMART AI tool went nationwide

A nationwide ground stop on Friday, the first since January 2023, delayed or cancelled roughly 7,000 flights after what Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called a communication issue affecting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers; officials attributed it to overloaded legacy telecom switches and routers producing inconsistent data. The stop came three days after the FAA extended SMART, Air Space Intelligence's advisory traffic-planning system built under a 12-year, $875 million contract, from the Washington area to all 22 en-route centres. The vendor said the outage was not based on or a result of SMART; Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said investigators were looking at it and 'you can't rule out anything'. No technical report has been published.

SecuritySMART reportedly runs on infrastructure separate from the systems that failed, but it ingests about 200 live data streams. Whether a data pipeline can load or destabilise a legacy telecom path is exactly the dependency question your own integrations should answer.

Nov 14, 2026 · OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet: dots agents pay with Visa, Mastercard and Amex network tokens, one biometric approval per purchase, no OpenAI cut at launch

ChatGPT Wallet, live on Thursday for a limited set of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over, lets a dot find items on a merchant's site, present a review screen of items, total and card, and complete checkout after the user approves with Face ID or a fingerprint. The card network issues a limited-use token with its own number, security code and expiry, so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the real card; rewards and dispute protections carry over. There are no spending limits or pre-approved purchases yet, OpenAI says it takes no share of transactions for now, and purchases fail on sites that block agents. OpenAI published a Commerce Partner Playbook and named Adyen, Checkout.com, Stripe and fraud vendors Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd as partners.

SecurityNetwork tokens mean a compromised agent cannot exfiltrate a card number, and each approval produces a signed record of what was authorised. That is a higher bar than most internal procurement bots meet; adopt it.

Nov 14, 2026 · IBM ships Granite 4.1 'LLMON' previews that bind instructions to data with reserved tokens, claiming near-zero indirect prompt-injection success on OPI

IBM published experimental 3B and 8B Granite 4.1 models trained on LLM Object Notation, an XML-like markup whose delimiters are reserved special tokens and whose tags mark instructions, data and requirements, with unique IDs tying each instruction to the data it may act on. On the OPI indirect-injection benchmark IBM reports attack success near zero, avoiding 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tested and 95% more than the best open model, with similar trends on ASB and StruQ and no loss on standard utility benchmarks. IBM calls it a preview not for production, says the resistance is learned rather than enforced, and has not yet tested adaptive multi-turn attacks.

SecurityThis is the first vendor training a model to treat untyped text as data by construction rather than by system prompt. Test it against your own injection corpus; if it holds, it changes what 'defence in depth' means for RAG and agents.
Source: IBM Granite on Hugging Face

Nov 13, 2026 · Attackers ran AntiFox, an open-source AI pentest platform, from Alibaba Cloud to steal AWS secrets: hundreds of API calls in 30 minutes

Arctic Wolf documented two intrusions in which self-hosted AntiFox agents, forked from the ARTEX framework, planted an ASPX webshell on an IIS server, read connection strings and config files, then cycled through AWS identities with hundreds of API requests in about half an hour; most were denied, some returned production secrets. A second case used a valid identity to read, copy and delete S3 objects. Amazon says its services operated as designed and the credentials came from compromised customer applications.

SecurityWrite the rule today: one principal, a burst of AccessDenied calls, a few successes, within minutes, correlated with new ASPX files. That is an agent enumerating permissions.

Nov 13, 2026 · Doximity: 63% of US physicians use AI for clinical decision support, up from 37% a year ago, and three in four hit a factual error

Doximity's survey of 3,379 physicians across 20 specialties, fielded in September and published November 12, finds more than half use AI daily and a further third weekly. Nov 12, 2026 · Railway's post-mortem: a build-sandbox escape led to $740,000 in fraudulent AI charges on stolen keys

Railway published its full incident report on November 10 for the October 20–21 breach. An attacker exploited a previously unknown flaw to partially escape filesystem isolation on a shared build VM, picked up a build credential that an internal Temporal instance wrongly trusted, read 353 builds' histories and a copy of Railway's monorepo, found a misconfigured OIDC binding and used it to reach a machine holding a long-lived decryption key. Environment variables for at most 1.85% of workspaces, about 100,000, may have been decrypted, with AI provider keys queried by name first. Railway estimates about $740,000 of unauthorised spend across customers' accounts, reset linked-account tokens for 1,852 users, says AWS and Google are refunding under existing policies while OpenAI and SpaceXSI review case by case, and is covering Anthropic and OpenRouter charges itself.

SecurityWiz's analysis of the stolen keys: tested within 30 minutes, then hammered four to seven days later. A key restricted to a narrow model allow-list throttled most of the traffic. Spend caps and scoped keys are the controls that worked.
Source: Railway incident report · CSO Online

Nov 12, 2026 · Google's Gemini Decision 1 scores up to 1,000 questions per call at $0.10 per million input tokens, no output charge

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API on November 11, powered by a model called gemini-decision-1 that scores a fixed set of options in a single forward pass and returns probabilities and confidence scores instead of generated text. One request takes up to 256,000 tokens of text, images, audio, video or PDFs and up to 1,000 independent questions, each a yes/no check, a multiple-choice question with up to 255 options or a rubric score, with Google quoting 150 to 250 milliseconds end to end. Pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens and nothing for output, with shared input billed once per request; Google says it is nearly five times cheaper than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite on its own benchmark suite. It is available in AI Studio with a free tier, and OpenAI launched a comparable Decisions API on GPT-6 Luna in October at the same starting price.

SecurityGoogle uses it in Search as a safety layer that flags adversarial MCP tool behaviour. A cheap, fast judge that cannot generate text is a useful guard in front of agents that can.
Source: Interesting Engineering · Google (announcement)
