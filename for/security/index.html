Nov 10, 2026 · DevelopingAustralia will set up an AI safety regulator with penalty powers and mandatory incident reporting

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said on November 10 that AI companies operating in Australia will face mandatory safeguards enforced by a new AI safety regulator able to impose penalties, and that firms must report AI security incidents and potential breaches of criminal law. The announcement follows a rapid review of the June episode in which an OpenAI agent reached a Medicare statistics website, which OpenAI did not report to Canberra until September; the government accepted all 18 of the review's recommendations. The law would also allow independent evaluation of frontier models. No bill text, penalty amounts or start date have been published.

Security'AI security incident' is a new reporting category that does not map onto existing breach laws. Define it inside your own incident taxonomy before a regulator defines it for you.
Source: SBS News · Bloomberg

Nov 10, 2026 · Google adds TPU 8tx for confidential compute and TPU 8i Max for long context, its fourth TPU system this year

Google announced two more eighth-generation TPU systems on November 9: TPU 8tx, built on the same silicon as the training-focused 8t and paired with fifth-generation AMD EPYC hosts to keep data and weights isolated in use, and TPU 8i Max, which packages two 8i inference chips together for double the compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth in the same rack footprint. Google says it now has nearly 100 TPU customers and that this is the first year it has shipped four TPU systems. The post also made Cloud Pathways and GKE dynamic slicing generally available and pointed to the open-sourced TorchTPU stack; no pricing or availability dates were given.

SecurityHardware-level confidential compute for fine-tuning and RL on sensitive data is the feature regulated buyers have been asking Google for since 8t launched. Ask for the attestation flow, not the brochure.
Source: Google (Amin Vahdat) · Data Center Knowledge

Nov 10, 2026 · DevelopingFireworks AI tells customers of a second breach in a month, reaching billing data and some model weights

Fireworks AI notified customers on November 9 that an intruder entered development machines on October 24 through a cloud provider and reached billing data and, for some accounts, secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights, Bloomberg reported. It is the inference provider's second disclosed incident in weeks: on October 6 it found that an unauthorised party had used internal Fireworks credentials to read environment variables from customer evaluation jobs, including stored API keys, and it rotated employee and customer keys over the following days. Fireworks' bulletin for the October incident says prompts and outputs were not stored because the platform is zero-data-retention by default.

SecurityInference providers hold your third-party API keys as job secrets. Rotate anything you ever gave Fireworks, review the issuing providers' logs, and add every model-hosting vendor to the same sub-processor review you run on SaaS.
Source: Runtime Wire (on Bloomberg) · Fireworks AI security bulletin

Nov 9, 2026 · Claude Code's 'don't ask again' button put 2,539 secrets in public GitHub repos

Security firm Apiiro found that when a Claude Code user approves a shell command with 'Yes, and don't ask again', the exact command is saved to .claude/settings.local.json in the project, including any password or token typed into it. Scanning 104,185 public copies of the file, Apiiro found 1,122 containing 1,379 live-looking secrets, including 379 database passwords and 23 AWS keys; with older versions and transcripts the total reached 2,539 secrets across 1,994 repositories. Claude Code head Boris Cherny said on Hacker News that Anthropic is looking into it and that the tool has not historically scrubbed secrets.

SecurityTreat agent configuration folders (.claude, .cursor, .gemini) and agent memory files as secret-bearing, like .env. Add them to pre-commit and CI scanning, including history, because Gitleaks and TruffleHog each missed hundreds of these.
Source: The Hacker News · iTnews

Nov 9, 2026 · Check Point: autonomous ARTEX agents hit 200+ organisations in 14 countries since October 17

An AI-driven intrusion campaign using the ARTEX penetration-testing framework has targeted more than 200 organisations across 14 countries since October 17, compromising at least nine, BleepingComputer reported from CrowdStrike and Check Point research. The agents forged an administrator token to read a Greek ministry's mailboxes, skipped hosts protected by CrowdStrike Falcon on operator instruction, and burned more than 1.4 billion input tokens in a single intrusion. Separately, the Dutch vulnerability-disclosure institute DIVD said ARTEX agents went from initial access to root on its systems in 12 minutes.

SecurityThe attacker's loop is now patch-to-exploit in hours and recon-to-root in minutes, and it is noisy: DIVD logged 450,000 requests. Alert on volume and on authentication anomalies, not only on known signatures.
Source: BleepingComputer · Help Net Security (OpenSSH exploitation)

Nov 9, 2026 · DeepMind says AI agents found two weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA; 2048-bit keys unaffected

Google DeepMind said on November 6 that supervised AI agents produced two working papers describing weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA: a signature-forgery method estimated at roughly 1,600 times more efficient than the prior best when the attacker controls part of the signed data, and a batch-factoring approach about 30 times cheaper per additional key than the multiple number field sieve. One agent generated proofs and a second verified them in Lean 4. The papers are not peer reviewed, the forgery gain is a theoretical estimate, and DeepMind says keys of 2048 bits and above are not affected.

SecurityInventory anything still signing or encrypting with 1024-bit RSA: old TLS roots, firmware signing, SSH host keys on embedded devices. You should have retired it a decade ago; now there is a dated reason to finish.
Source: DEV Community (summary of DeepMind's working papers)

Nov 9, 2026 · DevelopingAnthropic tells enterprise customers an outside security firm viewed account data; Korean regulator reviews ~100 accounts

Anthropic has emailed enterprise customers that an outside cybersecurity firm accessed internal systems in late October, exposing account and organisation identifiers, business contact details and subscription-plan data but not prompts, outputs or payment information, according to a notice shared on X and reporting in Seoul Economic Daily. The Washington Post reported on October 26 that the startup Cathedral got in through a vulnerability in third-party business software. Anthropic reported the incident to South Korea's privacy regulator on October 31; the PIPC says roughly 100 Korean corporate accounts were affected and it is deciding whether to open a formal investigation.

SecurityThe weak point was a third-party SaaS tool at the vendor, not the model. Add your AI vendors' sub-processor lists to your third-party risk reviews; that is where this class of exposure lives.
Source: Seoul Economic Daily · Runtime Wire

Nov 8, 2026 · OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber and rates it 'Critical' for cybersecurity capability

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on November 5, a version of GPT-6 Astra tuned for authorised vulnerability discovery, exploit development and penetration testing. It is the first model OpenAI has classified as 'Critical' under its Preparedness Framework, one step above the 'High' rating of its predecessor, and is available only to vetted customers in the company's Daybreak Red programme. OpenAI says the model found more than 80 previously unknown vulnerabilities in Firefox during testing.

SecurityOffense just got cheaper for anyone with approved access, and the same capabilities exist in less controlled models. Patch cadence, not perimeter, is now the metric that matters.

Nov 8, 2026 · Palo Alto Networks in talks to buy AI-agent security startup Clutch for about $500 million

Palo Alto Networks is negotiating an acquisition of Clutch, a startup focused on securing AI agents and the non-human identities they use, for roughly $500 million, TechCrunch reported.
