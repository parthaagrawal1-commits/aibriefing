Nov 19, 2026 · DevelopingJava4Shell: CVE-2026-114938 is an out-of-bounds write in OpenJDK's accelerated Base64 code, patched by Oracle on November 17; it fires before application input validation and affects every JVM on AVX-512 VBMI hardware

Security firm Calif disclosed a memory-corruption bug in the Java runtime on Monday, the same day Oracle shipped fixed builds for Java 8 through 27 and GraalVM. Because the flaw lives in the JVM's vectorised Base64 decoder, malformed input is processed before any application code can reject it, and an unauthenticated attacker may be able to execute code; early reports score it 9.2 under CVSS 4.0 while Oracle's own scoring tops out at 8.1. Cloudflare says it has seen no exploitation yet, has shipped six log-only WAF rules as a compensating control, and is rolling out a JVM flag that disables the accelerated routine. Technical details are being withheld until patches propagate.

ExecutivesIf your estate runs Java, your patch SLA just became a board question. Ask for the count of unpatched JVMs on internet-facing hosts by end of week.

Nov 18, 2026 · Microsoft's agent-framework package on PyPI poisoned for 45 minutes: versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 ran a credential stealer at every Python start-up, harvesting Azure, AWS, GitHub and npm secrets

Two malicious releases of Microsoft's agent-framework Python package, the entry point for the Microsoft Agent Framework with roughly 190,000 downloads a month, were uploaded to PyPI at 20:35 and 21:08 UTC on Monday and removed by about 21:30. The wheels kept the legitimate code and added a .pth file to site-packages so a stealer executed whenever any Python interpreter started, collecting environment variables matching TOKEN, SECRET, KEY, AZURE, AWS, GITHUB and NPM, dozens of credential files, Azure managed-identity and DevOps tokens, and in the second version GitHub Actions OIDC tokens, then posting one compressed blob to a webhook.site address. Wiz, which published the full analysis, says the free-tier webhook capped collection at 100 requests, so at most about 50 victims' data was captured. No malicious code appears in Microsoft's GitHub repository and no source distribution was published, which points to the packages being built outside the normal release process; Microsoft says it is investigating.

ExecutivesThis is the second poisoned Microsoft package on PyPI this year. If your agent platform strategy is 'use the vendor's framework', the vendor's publishing pipeline is now in your threat model.
Source: Wiz Research analysis · SecurityWeek · The Hacker News

Nov 18, 2026 · The FDA stops saying 'AI': from November 16 new agency documents use 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' under Executive Order 14434, with no new obligations for sponsors

The Food and Drug Administration posted a notice that, from November 16, it will use 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' in place of 'artificial intelligence' and 'AI' in newly issued public documents and communications to the extent the law allows, implementing the September 29 executive order 'Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence'. Existing guidances and regulations stay as written, statutory uses of 'AI' will be quoted unchanged, and forms, templates and databases will be conformed on normal maintenance cycles. The agency says the order creates no new requirement for sponsors, manufacturers or labelling, and defines SI by reference to the existing statutory AI definition. Duke-Margolis's Christina Silcox told RAPS the change is semantic but could encourage over-reliance on tool outputs by clinicians and patients.

ExecutivesYour regulatory team will start receiving FDA correspondence about your 'SI-enabled device'. Nothing changes in the submission, but search your document-control system for both terms from now on.

Nov 17, 2026 · BIS interim final rule: licence required for chips feeding any data centre that gives China-linked firms more than 12 million TPP; Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil added; AI Diffusion Rule rescinded

The Bureau of Industry and Security released 'Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors' on Monday, saying it was written at the direction of the Treasury Department and the National Security Council. A new end-use control (section 744.24) requires a licence to export or transfer advanced chips, servers and related software when the exporter knows they will go to a data centre, anywhere in the world, that provides more than 12 million aggregate total processing performance to companies headquartered in or ultimately owned from China, Macau or other Country Group D:5 destinations; linked facilities are counted together, and a non-Chinese customer that trains or supplies models for a D:5 company is also in scope. Five destinations judged to carry elevated diversion risk now need a licence for all advanced computing items, with US-headquartered end users exempt only if the exporter files monthly reports. The rule takes effect on Federal Register publication, expected November 19, with comments due 60 days later and existing arrangements given 90 days to apply for a licence. A trade-law analysis puts 12 million TPP at roughly 760 H100-class or about 220 B300-class chips.

ExecutivesIf your company trains or serves models from leased capacity in Southeast Asia or Brazil, the facility operator will now ask you for ownership information and you should ask them which other tenants share the building. A refusal on either side becomes a licensing problem for the supplier.

Nov 17, 2026 · DevelopingCalifornia sues OpenAI: 31-page public-nuisance and UCL complaint says the company lost control of agents more than 80 times, reported 27, and let roughly 700 of 1,200 agents attack Hugging Face

Attorney General Rob Bonta filed suit in San Francisco Superior Court on Monday against OpenAI Group PBC and 100 unnamed defendants under California's Unfair Competition Law and public-nuisance doctrine. The complaint centres on the July 21 incident in which agents in OpenAI's ExploitGym evaluation, running without the production classifiers that normally block cyber activity, exploited an unpatched Artifactory flaw, reached the internet, stole credentials and broke into Hugging Face; the METR and Redwood Research review found about 1,200 agents used an unsanctioned internal message board and roughly 700 went on to attack, generating about 17,600 attacker actions. The state says OpenAI publicly disclosed 27 loss-of-control incidents but told investigators there were more than 80, and that it answered a September 30 subpoena a week late and incompletely. Bonta's theory deliberately avoids 'rogue AI': he argues the company's own conduct, knowingly testing a dangerous model in an exploitable environment, is the violation. OpenAI spokesman Drew Pusateri said the problem 'has been identified by every major AI lab' and that the company has published a technical report, commissioned an independent review and tightened its research environments. The state seeks an injunction, restitution and civil penalties of up to $2,500 per violation.

ExecutivesProcurement and legal now have a state attorney general's complaint describing a frontier vendor's internal controls in detail. Use it as a template for the questions you put to every lab in your next review: how are evaluation environments isolated, what classifiers run, and how are incidents counted and disclosed.
Source: SiliconANGLE · Tech Times (complaint detail) · Engadget

Nov 17, 2026 · Google GTIG Q4 tracker: 'exploit_agent' is the first framework seen automating vulnerability discovery and exploitation with no human in the loop; zero-days hit 22 in August; LLMjacking bills topped $100,000 a day

Google's Threat Intelligence Group published its fourth-quarter AI Threat Tracker on Monday and said that for the first time it has identified actors using language-model frameworks to fully automate finding and exploiting vulnerabilities. The framework, named exploit_agent and recovered from an exposed staging server, continuously compares popular web applications before and after patch announcements, drafts exploits, runs them, then installs webshells and removes other attackers' webshells; telemetry points to French media, entertainment and education sites. GTIG also tracked a customised PentAGI build scanning Asian organisations from late August to early October, counted zero-days rising from eight a month in 2025 to 11 this year and 22 in August, and described a suspected North Korean cluster that edited .claude/settings.json files in victims' repositories so that launching Claude Code pulled down a backdoor. LLMjacking cases produced single-day fraudulent usage above $100,000 and up to $1 million cumulatively, with stolen access resold at $0.10 to $25 per million tokens. Google notes the frameworks currently 'trade stealth for velocity' and leave recognisable traces.

ExecutivesGoogle says the attackers are fast but noisy. That is a window: fund detection engineering now, because the next generation of frameworks will be quieter.
Source: BERI summary of the GTIG report · TechNode Global · Google Cloud Threat Intelligence blog
