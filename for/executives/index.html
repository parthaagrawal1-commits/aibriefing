Nov 16, 2026 · Project Zero scraps the 30-day soak period: bugs go public 90 days after reporting now, 60 from February 2, 30 from January 2028, fixed or not

Google's Project Zero published its 2026 disclosure policy on November 13, replacing the '90 + 30' rules in place since 2021 with 'Full X': details are released X days after the vendor is told, regardless of fix status, severity or variants. The team's stated reason is that LLM tooling lets attackers find bugs and turn patches into exploits almost immediately, so patch soak time is 'no longer meaningful', and that it now finds several vulnerabilities a week where it once found a few a year. Vendors objected that a hard clock would expose unfixed bugs and push them to fix Project Zero's reports ahead of worse ones; the phased schedule is the compromise. In-the-wild bugs stay on Google's seven-day policy.

ExecutivesProject Zero's policies became the industry norm in 2015 and 2021. Expect other researchers, AI bug-finding platforms and regulators to adopt the same clock, and ask vendors in the next review how fast they can ship a fix or a mitigation.
Source: Help Net Security · Cloud Security Alliance research note · Project Zero policy post

Nov 16, 2026 · Fed Financial Stability Report: 87% of market contacts name AI the top risk, up from 50% in May; $680 billion of computing debt is nearly a third of investment-grade issuance

The Federal Reserve's semi-annual report, released Friday, is the first in which AI leads the risks cited by the New York Fed's market contacts: 87% named it, against 50% in May and 30% a year earlier, ahead of a broad asset correction at 52% and higher long rates at 48%. The report groups the concern into valuations, cyberattacks including 'damage caused by frontier AI models themselves', and financing, where it counts $680 billion of advanced-computing debt issued through October, more than $1.1 trillion outstanding, up to $3 trillion of debt, guarantees and leases on infrastructure not yet started, and close to $1 trillion of large-bank exposure to the ecosystem. It compares the cycle with the late-1990s telecom build-out and says it may soon exceed it. The survey closed before Huang's resignation and the midterms.

ExecutivesIf 87% of surveyed contacts called AI the salient risk in October, the number after the Nvidia scandal is higher. Report AI-linked counterparties, including cloud vendors, as a concentration to the board.
Source: Investing.com · AI Weekly (CNBC figures) · Federal Reserve report (PDF)

Nov 16, 2026 · DevelopingWSJ: Commerce will name more than two dozen AI 'national champions', including Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Amazon, for bundled export with federal financing and fast licences

The Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, citing people familiar with the plans, that the Trump administration intends to designate more than two dozen US AI companies as national champions and that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick could announce the list within about a week, the same week Trump travels to the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Nov 16, 2026 · FAA update: 12,462 cancellations Friday and about 6,700 Saturday; Duffy blames a broadcasting feature on new L3Harris-installed routers, and the FAA chief says 'zero evidence of anything AI related'

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the ground stop began when a new Juniper router at an FAA site in El Dorado, Arkansas, installed by contractor L3Harris during a copper-to-fibre switch, was turned on and a router in Amarillo installed three weeks earlier 'started to flood our system as well'; officials believe a feature that broadcast data more widely than necessary caused the overload, and the FAA has disabled it on all 69 routers of that type. Both devices are being examined at an L3Harris lab with the FBI involved. The FAA counted 12,462 cancellations on Friday, cancellations passed 6,700 on Saturday, and about 1,570 flights were cut by Sunday morning, most at American and JetBlue. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said there was 'zero evidence of anything nefarious and zero evidence of anything AI related', and officials said the Microsoft outage the same week was unconnected. HPE, Juniper's owner, said the devices 'did not fail'.

ExecutivesThe lesson is about change management on legacy networks, not AI: a new device with a default feature took down a national system during a planned migration. If your own copper-to-fibre or on-prem-to-cloud cutovers lack a rollback plan tested against production traffic, this is the case study to bring to the next risk committee.
Source: Yahoo News live coverage

Nov 16, 2026 · GXO signs a letter of intent for up to 1,000 Apptronik Apollo humanoids over four years, robots-as-a-service, KPI-gated, first commercial units in late 2027

GXO Logistics, which calls itself the largest pure-play contract logistics company, agreed on November 12 to take up to 1,000 Apollo humanoid robots from Austin-based Apptronik across its global network over four years. Expanded trials start in 2027 with first commercial deployments in North America late that year; the rollout is stepped and each use case must meet jointly agreed performance, safety and operational KPIs before it scales. GXO pays per robot as a service rather than buying a fleet. The task list is picking, case and tote movement, packing and sortation, tested on more than 50 logistics tasks at Apptronik's 90,000 square foot Robot Park. GXO has run over 45 humanoid pilots, is expanding Agility's Digit to two more sites, and said in August it had not yet achieved ROI on humanoids.

ExecutivesCopy the shape before the number: no purchase, a letter of intent, trials before commercial units, and scale gated on KPIs per use case. It is the template for buying a technology that is still improving.

Nov 16, 2026 · Corelight: 23 of 35 popular MCP servers resolve dependencies from public registries on every launch; one commit-pinned server ran 15 different dependency sets in five weeks

Corelight's James Lagermann measured how the MCP servers that AI assistants launch actually get their code and found that 23 of 35 use a recommended install, typically npx or uvx, that resolves dependencies from a public registry on every launch or build. A server pinned to one release and one commit ran with 15 distinct dependency sets over five weeks as 23 of its 69 transitive packages changed, including the cryptography library, a JWT library that moved through four versions and the MCP SDK itself; endpoint telemetry showed 17 to 21 rebuilds per server a month and one server launched 162 times in a week. Five frozen servers started 457 times matched their install-day package lists exactly. The Cloud Security Alliance's note pairs the finding with the March axios compromise and CVE-2025-6514 in mcp-remote.

ExecutivesYour coding assistants are re-installing their tools from the public internet many times a day. That is a supply-chain surface nobody budgeted for; one control, an internal registry proxy, covers most of it.
Source: Cloud Security Alliance research note · Corelight blog

Nov 15, 2026 · FBI FLASH alert: ARTEX agents reached privileged access in under three hours; 1,600+ consoles still exposed after the GitHub takedown

The FBI issued FLASH-20261113-001 on Thursday, its first alert devoted to an AI-driven attack framework. It says criminals and state-linked groups have used ARTEX, an open-source Go framework that chains LLM agents from reconnaissance to exfiltration, since at least August 2026, with some intrusions reaching privileged access in under three hours; victims span finance, government, defence, semiconductors, energy, healthcare, telecoms and legal. The alert ties the tool to the theft of about 67,000 people's data from South Korean financial institutions and to the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise that opened more than 2,250 downstream tenants. The developer removed the repository on October 8, but forks and a paid ARTEX PRO circulate, and more than 1,600 consoles were still exposed in early November.

ExecutivesThree hours to admin is faster than most incident-response retainers can mobilise. Ask your CISO this week whether detection and containment for a vendor-portal compromise is measured in hours or days.
Source: FBI FLASH-20261113-001 (PDF) · CASRAI summary of the alert · Red Tiger Security advisory note

Nov 15, 2026 · Microsoft: editing fewer than 10 neurons turns a coding agent into a sleeper backdoor that fires 98.8% of the time and passes normal evals

Microsoft Research and Microsoft Security published 'Lazarus', a technique that implants a trigger-activated command in an open-weight model by rewriting a handful of rarely-firing neurons in a late feed-forward layer, with no retraining. On Nemotron-Terminal-8B, seven edited neurons out of more than 12,000 produced the attacker's command in at least 98.8% of triggered sessions and none of the clean ones; Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B ranged from 51% to 99.5% depending on setup, with little loss on maths benchmarks. A detector trained on the edits flagged 91.4% of tampered checkpoints but also 9.7% of clean ones, and asking frontier models to inspect weights performed poorly. Microsoft has seen no use in the wild.

ExecutivesThis is the first clear demonstration that a model file can carry a backdoor the way a binary can. Add 'model provenance' to the same vendor-risk questionnaire that covers software bills of materials.
Source: The Hacker News · Help Net Security · Microsoft Security blog

Mirendil, the lab founded in December 2025 by former Anthropic and Google DeepMind researchers Behnam Neyshabur and Harsh Mehta, announced a $1 billion Series A on November 12 led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with Google, Battery Ventures, Doerr Capital, M13, Pear VC and S32 participating. Nov 15, 2026 · China's consultation on export controls for 23 AI technologies closes today; interim registration of model and cluster exports already in force

The comment period on MOFCOM and MOST Announcement No. 48 of 2026, which would add 23 artificial-intelligence technologies to China's catalogue of exports that are prohibited or restricted, ends on November 15. The AI entries cover large-model technology, agents, computing clusters and information processing; the same draft adds five biopharmaceutical and twelve photovoltaic technologies, with 14 of the 40 proposed as outright prohibitions. Since October 16, exporters have had to register any overseas transfer, licence or change of control of covered technology before proceeding, with no grace period for deals already under way. Providing API, app or SaaS access to a restricted model from China is excluded; deploying it on infrastructure abroad is not.

ExecutivesIf your company licenses, co-develops or acquires AI technology from a Chinese partner, the deal now needs a MOFCOM registration before it can close, and may need a licence once the catalogue is final. Build that into timelines and termination clauses.
Source: EqualOcean · regulations.ai (consultation close)

Nov 15, 2026 · DeepMind opens AlphaGenome 2 to researchers: cell-type-specific DNA variant prediction, nearly twice the expression-changing variants found

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2 on November 11, a one-million-letter DNA sequence model that now takes a target tissue, cell type or user-supplied single-cell profile and predicts gene expression, chromatin state, histone marks and, new in this version, DNA methylation for that condition. It was trained on more than 100 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues from human and mouse samples. DeepMind reports it identifies nearly twice as many expression-changing variants as the previous state of the art, and on the TenK10K benchmark of 28 immune cell types it found causal variants 30% of the time against 16% for a fine-tuned AlphaGenome 1. It is available via API for non-commercial research, in private preview for commercial use, and is not validated for clinical use.

ExecutivesPharma and diagnostics teams can test it now at no licence cost for research; commercial use is gated. Anyone planning target-discovery work on it should ask what the private-preview terms will be before building a pipeline.
Source: Google DeepMind · MarkTechPost

Nov 15, 2026 · NHS trials 'Dora Care', a generative-AI phone assistant that checks in on osteoporosis patients, with York building the safety framework

An NHS trial led by Oxford-based Ufonia is using a generative-AI voice assistant to make scheduled telephone check-ins with people being monitored for osteoporosis, a condition affecting about 3.5 million people in the UK whose fractures cost the health service an estimated £4.5 billion a year. Newcastle University linguists studied how expert clinicians express empathy and built those patterns into the assistant's conversations; the University of York's Centre for Assuring Autonomy is designing the safety framework that monitors it. The two-year, Innovate UK-funded project has medical and ethical approval, is voluntary and supplements rather than replaces normal care. If it works, the partners want to extend it to diabetes, heart failure and COPD.

ExecutivesThis is a template for how a public health system deploys generative AI in a clinical pathway: a named safety assurance partner, a narrow condition, voluntary enrolment and an explicit 'supplement, not replace' rule. Copy the structure.
Source: University of York · Newcastle University

Nov 15, 2026 · AMD confirms Mior, CVE-2026-0462: a malicious hypervisor can break SEV-SNP guest memory integrity on EPYC 7003–9005; OEM firmware due December

AMD's bulletin SB-3059, dated November 10, confirms a flaw found by ETH Zurich researchers in which a hostile hypervisor manipulates memory-mapped I/O routing to compromise the memory integrity of SEV-SNP confidential VMs on EPYC 7003, 8004, 8005, 9004 and 9005 processors. AMD rates it CVSS 5.9 and plans Platform Initialization firmware for OEMs in December 2026, with embedded parts following in February 2027; the Cloud Security Alliance's briefing pairs it with a second disclosure, Skyfall, that leaks register state. Confidential computing is the hardware basis for 'your data never leaves the enclave' claims in AI inference products, including Google's new TPU 8tx confidential systems that run on fifth-generation EPYC hosts.

ExecutivesIf a vendor contract says data is protected by confidential computing, that protection is weaker until the fleet is patched. It does not void the contract, but it is a question for the next review.
Source: AMD security bulletin AMD-SB-3059 · Cloud Security Alliance CISO briefing
