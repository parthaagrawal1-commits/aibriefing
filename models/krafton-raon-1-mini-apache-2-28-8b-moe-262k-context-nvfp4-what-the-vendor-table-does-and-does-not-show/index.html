KRAFTON's Raon-1 mini: a 28.8B Apache-2.0 MoE with 262K context that leads its weight class on the vendor's table and trails a dense 27B on most of it

The full release of Korea's games-publisher-built model adds hybrid linear attention, six reasoning-effort levels and a 16 GiB NVFP4 checkpoint; the benchmarks are self-reported and judged by GPT-5.6 Luna.

Why it matters
ML engineersThe architecture (18 linear-attention layers, 7 gated MLA layers, 8 KiB KV per token) is the interesting part. The scores are vendor-run; re-run the three you use.
DevelopersApache 2.0, GGUF and MLX builds, and a 5090 profile. The card's own VRAM claims conflict (20 GiB vs 24 GB), so test the context length you need.
ExecutivesA games publisher shipping a competitive open model says more about the falling cost of the mid tier than about KRAFTON's AI strategy.

KRAFTON, the Korean publisher behind PUBG, posted the full Raon-1 mini on Hugging Face this week under an Apache 2.0 licence, replacing the preview it released on October 21. The Elec dates the release to November 25; the repository's metadata says November 24. The company says the model was designed and trained from scratch across seven languages, with a 128-expert mixture-of-experts of 28.8B total parameters, 4.3B active per token across six routed experts plus one shared, and a 262,144-token context window. It ships with six reasoning-effort levels from none to max, defaulting to high.

The architecture is the news

The model's 25 layers are split between 18 layers of KRAFTON's own linear attention, which it calls Raon Delta Attention, and 7 layers of gated multi-head latent attention. The practical consequence is memory: the MLA layers keep about 8 KiB of KV cache per token in BF16, and the card says a full 256K-token request fits in 24 GB of VRAM. The highlights section says 20 GiB, which is a conflict the card does not resolve. The RTX 5090 profile uses a 32K maximum model length; the vLLM example was tested on a B300. NVFP4 weights come to 16.3 GiB, and MXFP8, GGUF and MLX builds are available, along with a DFlash2 draft model for speculative decoding. KRAFTON says the linear attention is what makes the model fast and cheap to serve, which is the same bet Qwen3-Next and MiniMax made earlier in the year.

What the table shows

KRAFTON's comparison set is Qwen3.6 35B-A3B, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Flint, Inkling 2 Mini and the dense Qwen3.8 27B. Against the MoE peers, Raon-1 mini leads on GPQA Diamond (86.0 against 83.2 and 80.7), MultiChallenge (64.9, up 5.9 from the preview), BrowseComp Plus (55.9) and GDPval (72.3). On AIME 2026 it scores 96.7 and on HMMT February 87.1. Against Inkling 2 Mini it is roughly level on GPQA (86.6) and AIME (97.5) and well behind on Terminal-Bench Lite, 52.0 to 77.0. Against the dense Qwen3.8 27B it trails almost everywhere: 89.7 on GPQA, 98.8 on AIME, 58.0 on BrowseComp Plus. On a full 2027 Korean CSAT exam it scores 371.8 of 450, below both Gemma 4 (392.3) and Qwen3.8 (400.2), which is an odd result for a Korean-first model and one the card does not explain.

The footnotes matter. The judge and user simulator for every evaluation is GPT-5.6 Luna; GDPval is a judge score rather than an Elo; AutomationBench uses the public split; the Korean chatbot benchmark is internal. All numbers are developer-reported and the technical report promised at the preview has still not appeared. Chip Dispatch notes that Aleph Alpha's Kolibri 1.1 and inclusionAI's Ling-3.2-air shipped the same day, so there are three new open MoEs in this weight class to compare, none independently evaluated yet.

Why a games company

KRAFTON's stated reason is cost and control for its own products, which need Korean, Japanese and English at low latency. The broader story is the Korean government's sovereign-AI push, which has funded national-champion model efforts at LG, Naver, SK and Upstage, and now has a games publisher shipping something competitive on a shoestring. The Elec reports that KRAFTON plans a larger model and a real-time bidirectional speech model next, and that an API and test environment are available.

What to watch

  • Independent numbers. Artificial Analysis and the open leaderboards will run Raon-1 mini within days; the gap between those and the card is the first thing to check.
  • The technical report. Training data, token count and the linear-attention derivation are all unpublished; without them the from-scratch claim is unverifiable.
  • The 256K-on-24GB claim. If it holds, this is the cheapest long-context agent you can run on one consumer GPU.
  • Whether the three same-day open MoEs converge on hybrid linear attention. If they do, the architecture question for 2027 small models is settled.

Key terms in this story

  • Linear attention An attention variant whose cost grows in proportion to sequence length rather than with its square, trading some recall for cheap long contexts.
  • Context window The maximum text a model can consider in one request: prompt, documents, conversation and answer combined.
  • Evaluation (eval) A test set with known-good answers used to measure a model or system on your own tasks.
  • Benchmark A standard test used to compare models, such as a set of coding tasks or exam questions.
  • Token The unit models read and write; roughly three-quarters of an English word. Pricing and context limits are counted in tokens.
