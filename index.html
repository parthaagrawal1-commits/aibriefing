Nvidia posts $115.8 billion and guides to $130 billion with no China compute revenue assumed; data centre up 110% to $107.6 billion, margins to fall to 71–72%, $390 billion of purchase commitments

Nvidia's third quarter, the first reported by interim chief executive Colette Kress, came in at $115.8 billion of revenue, up 103% on the year and about $7 billion above consensus, with non-GAAP earnings of $2.68 a share against $2.47 expected and a 74.9% gross margin. The fourth-quarter guide of $130 billion plus or minus 3% includes an extra week, carries a gross-margin outlook of 71% to 72% as memory costs rise, and explicitly assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. Purchase commitments rose to $390 billion, neocloud commitments to $32 billion, and the company returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter with $227 billion of buyback authorisation left. Chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to bring Jensen Huang back and will not rush a permanent appointment; the shares rose about 4% after hours from their lowest close since June 2025.

The one thing to know today

If any machine, container or CI runner installed Microsoft's agent-framework Python package between 20:35 and 21:30 UTC on November 17, treat every secret it could reach as stolen: search lockfiles and images for versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2, look for agent_framework_meta.pth or af_runtime_diag.pth in site-packages, and rotate Azure, AWS, GitHub and npm credentials on the host. The stealer ran at interpreter start-up, so importing the package was not required.
