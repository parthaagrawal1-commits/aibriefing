Nvidia's board met to decide Huang's future; shares fell 8.6% after hours and 6% premarket

The Wall Street Journal reported on November 11 that Nvidia's board was preparing to remove Jensen Huang as chief executive, and Reuters reported that the independent directors met late on Wednesday to decide. Bloomberg reported the board is working with an outside firm on possible replacements, including an interim chief, with Seeking Alpha naming CFO Colette Kress as a possibility. The New York Times separately reported, citing five current and former employees, that Huang had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's healthcare vice-president, who reports to him; Nvidia did not comment. Shares closed up 2.9% at $173.73, fell 8.6% to $158.78 after hours and were down about 6% in Thursday premarket trading, roughly 27% below the early-October peak. No decision had been announced by publication, and Nvidia reports earnings on November 17.

The one thing to know today

Railway's post-mortem is the clearest account yet of how stolen AI keys turn into cash: tested within 30 minutes, flooded with traffic four to seven days later, and the only key that held up was one scoped to a narrow set of models. Put a spend cap and a model allow-list on every production API key today; the rotation can wait until tomorrow, the cap cannot.
