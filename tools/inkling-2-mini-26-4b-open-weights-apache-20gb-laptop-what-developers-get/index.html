Inkling 2 Mini: a 26.4B open-weight model that runs its middle layers three times, fits in 20 GB and scores 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified. What developers get, what it costs on Tinker, and where it falls short

Thinking Machines' smallest model is the first mainstream release built around looping rather than depth, and it lands on a Mac with 32 GB of memory under an Apache 2.0 licence.

Why it matters
DevelopersA permissively licensed coding agent that runs locally at roughly 20 GB changes the default for on-laptop and on-prem agents. Test it on your own repository, not the leaderboard: the weak spots are multi-turn instruction following and prompts with many constraints.
ML engineersThe architecture is the story: 24 stored layers, 48 applications per token, experts pruned mid-training from 384 to 128 per layer, native NVFP4 for most of the network. It is a template for getting large-model behaviour out of small-model memory.
FinanceNo serverless API price yet; Tinker sampling is $0.50 per million output tokens. For most teams the relevant cost is hardware they already own.

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini on Friday, the third and smallest member of its Inkling 2 family and the first that most developers can run on the machine in front of them. The weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, with day-one support in Ollama for Macs with at least 32 GB of memory, on Nvidia's DGX Spark, and in SGLang, vLLM, llama.cpp and Unsloth GGUF builds. The checkpoint is about 20 GB.

The architecture

The headline number is 26.4 billion total parameters with 3.4 billion active per token, but the interesting figure is 48. The model stores 24 layers in four blocks of six. The first and last blocks run once per token; the two middle blocks run three times, so each token passes through 48 layer applications while the memory footprint is that of 24. Counting the loops, the company gives 5.4 billion weight activations per token and uses that figure in its comparison tables. Training started with 8 of 384 experts active per layer, pruned to 128 experts per layer partway through, then ran about a trillion tokens of distillation and reinforcement learning over 2.2 trillion tokens. Twenty of the 24 layers are stored natively in NVFP4, the remaining four in MXFP8, which is why the file is so small. A research variant with a variable number of loops ships alongside it.

The numbers

Thinking Machines claims the best open-weight results under 100 billion total parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 at 48.2% and the Vals Index at 46.5%. It reports 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified, 77.9% on the hard split of SWE-bench Pro v2, 71.6% on Terminal Bench 2.1 against 73.0% for Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B, 79.0% on BrowseComp, 82.7% on MCP Atlas, and 84.5% and 49.3% on ARC-AGI versions 1 and 2. On the Artificial Analysis index it scores 20.1, tied with Qwen3.8-27B. The company is candid about the weaknesses: 50.0% on MultiChallenge and 33.4% on ComplexConstraints, both tests of following long multi-turn or heavily constrained instructions, which is exactly where a looped small model would be expected to struggle. The blog does not publish a head-to-head table against Inkling 2 Small; it says Mini approaches Small on a hard GPU-programming set and on a controllability measure.

Two caveats for anyone reading these as purchasing data. All figures are self-reported and the agentic ones depend on scaffolding. And the model card lists a 32 GB hardware floor even though the checkpoint is 20 GB, because activations and the KV cache for its 1 million token context need room.

What it costs

There is no serverless inference price for Mini yet; Thinking Machines' serverless tier still lists Inkling 2 Small at $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.00 output with a 256K context. On the Tinker training platform, Mini is priced at $0.15 per million prefill tokens, $0.015 cached, $0.50 per million sampled tokens, and $0.75 or $1.50 per million training tokens depending on whether you use the 64K or 256K context option. We have added those rates to the pricing tracker with a note that they are Tinker sampling rates rather than an API list price.

What to do

If you run coding agents on developer laptops or inside a VPC, this is the model to benchmark this week against Qwen3.8-27B and IBM's Granite 4.1 on your own tasks, with attention to how it handles long conversations and multi-step instructions. If you fine-tune, the Tinker route gives you a looped architecture without writing a training loop. And if you ship it, read the Apache 2.0 notice obligations once; they are light, but they exist, and the open-source licences explainer on this site covers the differences from the Llama-style licences you may be used to.

Key terms in this story

  • Distillation Training a smaller model to imitate a larger one's outputs, capturing much of its capability at lower cost.
  • Inference Running a trained model to produce output. What you pay for when you call an API.
  • Benchmark A standard test used to compare models, such as a set of coding tasks or exam questions.
  • KV cache The stored keys and values a transformer computes for every token in the context, kept in GPU memory so the model does not reprocess the whole prompt at each step.
  • Token The unit models read and write; roughly three-quarters of an English word. Pricing and context limits are counted in tokens.
